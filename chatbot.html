FAQs about Privacy in Conversational Agents (i.e., Chatbots)

Q1. What types of information are generally considered private and may pose privacy risks? +
•  Private Information Disclose Sensitive Details about You or Others +

Privacy experts generally consider information private if it can directly or indirectly reveal your or others’ identities or disclose sensitive details about your or others’ lives and behavior. When such information is linked to each other and exposed to attackers, it can be misused or cause harm to you and/or others.

Find more details Here and Here.

•  Examples of Private Information and How They Could Be Misused +

For example, information such as full name, date of birth, social security number, physical address, email address, and phone number can more directly reveal an individual’s identity. This type of information is known as Personally Identifiable Information (PII) and is considered extremely sensitive. Other private information includes details that can disclose sensitive aspects of people’s lives and behaviors, such as financial records, health information, location data, and social relationships.

An example of misusing such data is if an attacker gains access to your private conversations with chatbots and combines that information with your email address or full name shared on other platforms like educational websites, they could forge your identity, potentially leading to identity theft or financial fraud.

Find more details Here and Here.

Q2. Am I completely anonymous (i.e., my conversations and my identity cannot be linked and accessed) when interacting with chatbots like ChatGPT? +
•  Chatbots Cannot Completely Guarantee Your Anonymity +

When using chatbots like ChatGPT, you might feel anonymous since the chatbot doesn't know who you are or cannot judge you by what you share. However, your anonymity isn't guaranteed. There have been cases [Read More Here] where individuals' identities were linked to their sensitive information and exposed to others.

•  AI Can Link Your Information from Various Sources +

A 2024 study shows that AI technologies, including conversational agents (i.e., chatbots), have increased privacy risks. Traditionally, your information online has been scattered across different sources, making it difficult for attackers to connect all those pieces to your identity. But, modern chatbots are trained on diverse data, including yours, from various sources on the internet—such as text, images, and audio files from social media, personal websites, email services, and educational platforms. This means that if unauthorized people gain access to this training data—as it is shown to be possible [Read More Here and Here]—they could link your information across the web, potentially revealing more about you than you intended.

•  Third-parties Could Access Your Data +

Your data could be accessed by third parties [See Examples Here and Here], such as other companies or legal authorities, who may have different privacy policies than the company that owns the chatbot you're using. This means they could potentially link your shared data to your identity. So, while using these chatbots, remember that anonymity isn't guaranteed, and privacy risks can be further exacerbated.

Q3. Do I need to sacrifice my privacy (e.g., sharing my private information) or others’ privacy (e.g., sharing my friends’ information) to get the most out of chatbots like ChatGPT? +
•  Don’t See Privacy as All-or-nothing +

Chatbots can assist you without needing sensitive details. Also, decisions about your privacy when interacting with chatbots shouldn’t be seen as all-or-nothing. You can avoid sharing unnecessary information that poses privacy risks but doesn’t significantly improve the chatbot's utility. For example, disclosing personally identifiable information—e.g., names, birthdates, or physical addresses—usually doesn't enhance the chatbot’s effectiveness much but does increase privacy risks. For most tasks, you can protect your privacy and still benefit from the chatbot.

•  Assess and Consider the Risks Before Sharing Information +

If a task requires private information, consider whether it's worth the risk. Ask yourself about the risks associated with sharing private information. If you're unsure, it might be safer not to use the chatbot for that task. You can also use generalized or fictional information instead of exact details—like stating a country or city instead of an exact physical address.

•  Respect Others’ Privacy +

Be cautious about sharing others' private information, as their privacy preferences and contexts may differ. Without their consent, it is safer to avoid sharing their details with chatbots to respect their privacy.

Q4. I’ve already shared my private information on other platforms (e.g., social media). How does sharing this information with chatbots like ChatGPT affect my privacy? +
•  Sharing with More Platforms May Mean Greater Privacy Risks +

Each time you share information on a new platform, you increase the chances of it being accessed, aggregated, or linked to other data. This makes it easier for someone to piece together a more complete picture of your identity. Traditionally, your data is sparse and scattered across different resources, but newer platforms like conversational agents (i.e., chatbots) make it easier to connect these pieces of information about you [Read more Here], raising potentially serious privacy risks.

•  Chatbots Process Data Differently +

Chatbots use your information differently than other platforms like online websites or social media. While online websites or social media might store and display your data, chatbots process it to ‘generate’ responses, which could involve storing or using the data in ways you might not expect.

•  Chatbots Can Be Major Sources of Individuals’ Private Information +

As more individuals share private information with chatbots, these tools become significant sources of personal data, increasing the severity of potential data breaches. This concern is heightened by recorded incidents [See examples Here, Here, and Here] in which different chatbots have exposed users' private information.

Q5. Is it the responsibility of the companies and developers of chatbots (e.g., ChatGPT) to protect my private information or is it my responsibility? +
•  Protecting Your Private Information is a Shared Responsibility +

Companies and developers of chatbots are responsible for ensuring that your data is secure from unauthorized access and for clearly communicating how your data is collected, used, stored, and shared. On your end, it's important to regularly evaluate the risks and benefits of sharing your private information and make informed decisions about what to share and what to withhold.

•  Chatbots’ Privacy Risks and Measures Are Not Fully Understood Yet +

It is important to know that once you share your data, you expose yourself to privacy risks that may be unknown to you and/or the companies at the moment. Even with the best intentions, companies and developers may not be able to prevent all privacy risks, as many are still being discovered [Here]. This is partly due to the complex, opaque nature of modern chatbots, which even experts struggle to fully understand [Add link/s].

•  Unclear Privacy Risks and Protections Pose Future Potential Threats +

The unclear privacy risks and protections in conversational agents (i.e., chatbots) mean that what you share now could be used in harmful ways in the future. You can help protect your own and others’ privacy by avoiding or minimizing the sharing of sensitive information with chatbots, especially when they are not even necessary for the chatbot’s utility.
